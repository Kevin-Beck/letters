# TBF1 Navy Letters: WWII Letters of Ens. Thomas Frazier Beck

This folder holds scanned letters written by **Thomas Frazier "Tom" Beck** of
Karns City, Pennsylvania during his U.S. Navy service in World War II
(1942–1945). Most are to his mother, Elva Beck (Mrs. John A. Beck). There are
also a few family letters, a telegram, V-mail forms, and a newspaper clipping.

In September 2026 every scan was read and typed out, so the letters can be
read, searched, and shared without the images.

## What's in this folder

| Location | What it is |
|---|---|
| `*.jpg` (this folder) | Original scans of the letters |
| `TBF1Adobe/Adobe finished/` | A second, cleaned-up set of scans (often clearer) |
| `TBF0defective/` | Loose pages that were missing the rest of their letter |
| `Tom Beck/` | Family photographs (not letters; not transcribed) |
| **`transcriptions/`** | **The typed transcriptions and the index (see below)** |

## The transcriptions

`transcriptions/` contains:

- **116 text files**, one per letter, named by date and recipient. For
  example, `1943-01-24_Tom_to_Mother.txt`. Sorting by name puts them in
  date order. Undated items start with `Undated_`.
- **`letters_index.json`**, an index of every letter. For each one it
  lists the original image files, who wrote it, who it was to, the date and
  how that date was worked out, the location, whether any pages are
  missing, and notes on the people and events it mentions. It also includes
  a short family guide, a timeline of Tom's service, and a list of every
  duplicate or misfiled scan.
- **`annotations/`**, background notes on the ships, people, places,
  events and wartime terms in the letters, for hover text in a future app
  (like Wikipedia's blue links). The transcriptions themselves are unchanged.
  See `transcriptions/annotations/README.md`.

Each text file starts with a header (date, source images, completeness),
followed by the letter, page by page.

### How the transcriptions were written

- Spelling and grammar are kept exactly as written.
- `[?]` marks a word that was hard to read, and `[illegible]` marks one that
  couldn't be read.
- `[crossed out]` marks text Tom struck through.
- `[bracketed letters]` are letters cut off at the edge of a scan.
- A few letters contain racist or antisemitic language that was common at
  the time. It has been kept as written, as part of the historical record,
  and each affected file has a note at the top.

## Things to know about the scans

- **Many filenames have the wrong date.** Filenames look like `YYMMDD`
  (e.g. `430118pg1` = Jan 18, 1943), but a number were misdated or misfiled.
  For example, `420716*` is really a November 1942 letter, and `451116*` is a
  copy of a 1942 letter. Each transcription is dated from the letter itself,
  and the index explains how.
- **Most pages were scanned more than once**, often with different names in
  different folders. The clearest scan was used, and the others are listed in
  the index under `duplicate_and_misfiled_scans`.
- **Pages from different folders were joined up.** Several letters were
  missing pages in one folder but complete once all three were combined.
- **Five letters are still incomplete** because some pages were never
  scanned (for example, the backs of two sheets of the April 15, 1943
  letter). They are marked `"complete": false` in the index. If the original
  letters still exist, those pages could be rescanned.

## Tom's service, in brief

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| July 1942 | Enters the Naval Reserve Midshipmen's School at Columbia University, New York |
| Oct 21, 1942 | Commissioned Ensign at Riverside Church |
| Nov 1942 | Amphibious training at Norfolk, VA and Solomons, MD |
| Jan 1943 | Houston, TX; his first ship, USS LCI(L) 345, is commissioned |
| Feb 1943 | Becomes executive officer of **USS LCI(L) 62** and sails for the South Pacific |
| 1943 – early 1944 | Serves in the South Pacific (details censored in the letters) |
| Spring 1944 | Returns to the United States |
| 1944 – 1945 | Shore duty at the U.S. Naval Station, Portland, Maine |
| Spring 1945 | Hospitalized with malaria; writes home on V-E Day |

The index has a fuller timeline and notes on the family members who appear in
the letters.
