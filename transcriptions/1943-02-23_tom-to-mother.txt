                                                  Feb. 23, 1943
Dear Mother,
     No change of address for once and also no other news of interest. We are
anchored out to-night and I think for the last time for quite sometime. I
believe we will be able to stow the anchor below and forget about it. Of course
the Navy has changed its mind before and can do it again.

     Some of the crew cut their hair with clippers to-day and the[y] really
look peculair. A couple of them went further and shaved their heads and they
look worse. The top of their head is white and their face is brown. When we get
outside of land and there are no chances of turning back I think I will put the
clippers to mine. If I come back bald headed you will know that my hair

[--- 1943-02-23_tom-to-mother_p02.jpg ---]

forgot to grow back in. I am going to try to clip the skipper when we cross the
equator. It wouldn't make much difference because he is just about bald now.

     Tell John I am enjoying Balzac and I have it just about finished. I
thought I would have time to read when underway but found out that is
impossible. There is too much work to be done.

     Reveille is at 0500 to-morrow so had better close and get in my sack. It
is quite possible that this will be the last letter for awhile.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom

P.S. Don't worry if you don't hear from me for a couple of months.
