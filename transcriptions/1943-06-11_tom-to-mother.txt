                                                  June 11, 1943
Dear Mother,
     Yesterday morning I went ashore for some supplies and the trip was longer
than I expected. I was waiting on the dock for a boat back when the boat for
the Officer's Club pulled up. I put the stuff in the boat and joined the rest.
On the way we stopped for mail and I received two from you. Both were recent,
May 28, & 31. In them were Jackie's pictures, Henry's letter, and John's
announcement. I really had a few very enjoyable minutes - sitting under a
coconut tree drinking beer and reading mail from home.

     I wrote Henry last night and decided to wait until to-day to answer your
letters.

     It is too bad the rain is holding up the spring planting. There is still

[--- 1943-06-11_tom-to-mother_p02.jpg (marked "-2-") ---]

plenty of time left for gardens. The rain is the same all over the world - it
makes every thing so dismal and every one so disagreeable. The work goes much
smoother when the sun shines.

     The war news is doing a lot to cheer us up. The Allies seem to be doing
well on all fronts and, by the news from Washington and London, we will doing
much better shortly. It is only a matter of time and time passes quickly out
here.

     It must have been a relief to all to hear from Henry. Africa shouldn't be
too dangerous now.

     So John has made his intentions known to the public. He is caught now and
there isn't much he can do about it. I would like to be home for the wedding
but that is impossible. He probably stick me for a wedding present any way but
