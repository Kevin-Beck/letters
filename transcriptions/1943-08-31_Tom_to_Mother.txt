                                                  August 31, 1943
Dear Mother,
     Another month has flown by and that probably means something although I
don't know just what. I think what makes the days go so fast is that I seldom
know the day or the date. There are no week ends and that makes a big
difference. Joe and I often argue about the date and I have missed as high as
four and five days.

     August was a big month for you. Getting a daughter and having a birthday.
Do you feel any older now? You know life begins at 40 so when I get home I'll
expect you to help me let the county know. Start hoarding the red paint.

     It looks like I was all wet when I disputed you judgement about the
succession of the matrimonial line.

[--- 430831pg2.jpg (marked "-2-") ---]

I thought Bill had more resistance than that. On second thought, it would take
a lot once Janny has her mind set. My best wishes to the conquerer.

     The enclosed item is to be forwarded to that financial wizard, my adviser,
for disposal. It is up to him as to the best way to get rid of it. That is what
I am paying for. Of course his compensation depends directly upon the results.
One big reason he has the job is that in my study of higher economics I
remember only one law - the law of diminishing returns.

     Mac was sweet to me and wrote a letter. I really appreciated it and he was
the only member of the family that I haven't corresponded with before. Betty
has been very good in writing

[--- 430831pg3.jpg ---]

to me but I guess I yelled too loud at John so he broke down. I also expect a
few letters from the new Beck family. I am away off the subject I started on.
Mac offered to send me more tobacco but that isn't necessary. While living off
the Army we received every thing. When they issued rations it includes not
only food but toilet articles, candy, [crossed out] tobacco (cigarettes, pipe,
chewing, makings for roll your own). I have about 15 tins of pipe tobacco now.
Besides I can't buy plenty here and it is cheaper. But, pipes are scarce. So
tell old money bags to dig deep and if I have a few dollars left to buy a
couple of pipes. Mac is a good judge of pipes. If he needs a letter to mail
them let me know and I will have one typed.
     It really give me a grand

[--- 430831pg4.jpg (marked "4"; top line partly cut off) ---]

feeling to hear that Plink [?] asks about me. I am not sure if I told you this
before and if so I shall repeat. I have often thought of asking for his address
but the length of time it would take for a letter to get to him and back here
seems to make writing foolish. If you can get his address please send it to me
because some day I will probably change my mind. I wrote to Henry a couple of
months ago and haven't received an answer yet.

     Today was pay day and I went ashore to get the money for the crew. I did
that the first thing in the morning and then spend the rest of the day touring
around trying to get a few things. I had an uneasy feeling all the time and
was relieved when I paid the men and everything checked. I was carrying
$1685.00. Thank God

[--- 430831pg5.jpg (marked "5") ---]

I am not a disbursing officer. I left $540 on the books and don't intend to
draw it until it is necessary. I will need some for mess bill at the end of
October. The rest is for you and me to spend on that 30 day leave I will get
next year. I might save some for Mari - a dollar or two. The check is for
travel between Norfolk & Houston.

     I went native and cut the toes and sides out of a pair of shoes. My
[crossed out] toes now resemble my nose in color - red as a beet and about ten
times as sore. The shoes are good for rainy weather as the rain can drain out
of them now.

     Tell Izzy I send my sympathy and also I wish her the best of luck in
hunting this season or is there any season on the game she is stalking.

     Business & health as usual here.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom

[Written at bottom left:] BECK, T.F. ENS.
