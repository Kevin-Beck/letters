                                                  Nov, 1942
                                                  Sunday
                                                  [later hand: 11/16/42?]
Dear Mother & Dad,
     I related before my experience on the first trip out in the bay. The
L.C.T. (landing craft - tanks) is built like a barge. It is about 105 feet
long and on the last 15' is a poop-deck or fantail or counter, all about the
same thing, on which is the wheel house. The conning tower is the small space
in front of the wheel house. The man in charge "takes the con" and gives
orders to the helmsman & engineer. The engines are controled just like John's
boat, - there are three levers for [the, inserted] three engines - ahead,
forward, middle - neutral, back - reverse. There is a throttle for each engine
& a r.p.m. dial. I understand this ship is the only one in the Navy that can
turn on its heel. The bow can be turned any direction & the stern will remain
stationary.

     On the second a day I had a turn at the wheel and controls. I also had
the con for awhile and all I did was turn around and tried to keep a course
on some point - It was just

[--- 421116pg2.jpg ---]

practice and didn't go anywhere in particular. In the afternoon I went out,
sat around and got cold. When we were told we had to go out again that evening
I was sure I would freeze.

     It was dark when we started out and when we cleared the channel the
skipper called us to-gether and asked us to describe all light we had passed.
On the way out I tried to remember each one but had no idea he would ask us
about them. We were huddled to-gether when the skipper asked,
"Who is taking the con?"
Silence covered the bay and then in a hesitant voice I answered, "I'll try."
"Good," he said, "Where are you going?"
"I don't know."
"Well, head for that light."
"Aye, aye, sir," I replied and then gave the orders,
"Both engines ahead 10,000. Rudder left 15." We [were] only using the two
outboard motors. Every thing was going fine until the skipper barked,
"Head for Fish Point"
Having no idea where Fish Point is I looked at him and then,
"Where do you think it is?"

[--- 421116pg3.jpg (marked "-2-") ---]

I pointed and he grunted "Good." With little difficulty I was head on for Fish
Point. Out of the darkness I heard a bellow, "What is that on your port
quarter?"
I ran around the wheel house, upon seeing some moving lights I replied, "A
ship, sir."
"Which way is it going?" I pointed.
"Which way are we going?" Again I pointed.
"What is going to happen?"
"We are going to collide, sir." There was a quick pause and suddenly the air
was split with "For ---- ----, do something."
"Aye, Aye, sir." Then "Rudder hard right."
We got back on a course when the same questions were asked. This ship was
approaching on our starboard bow. Answering his questions satisfactory, he
tried a fast one.
"Who is the burdened vessel"
"We are, sir."
"Good. Now try a hard left rudder."
Complying with his orders and pulling straight away from the other ship he
said,
"Now what happen?"
"He is the burdened vessel."
"Good."
     For awhile he gave me various courses

[--- 421116pg4.jpg ---]

to follow and a few turns to make. Suddenly he stopped both engines & put the
rudder amidships.
Looking at me he said, "Now, take her home."
I ran around the wheel house looking for a flashing red light and found it
dead astern. That marked the channel I wanted. I barked, "Port engine astern
10, starboard ahead 10, rudder hard left."
     There was a brisk wind and the bow was swing into it not making much
progress. When the turn was about completed I gave, "Port ahead 10." Then,
"Meet her" but too late I had swung past the course and instantly I heard,
"Where the hell are you going?"
"I didn't meet her, sir."
A faintly audible grunt was all I heard.
     The ship was finally on it course to the buoy when the skipper came up and
said, "O.K., Mister. I'll take her now." Turning to the wheel house he yelled,
"Both engines ahead 21,000. Let get out of here - damn cold as hell."
     That was my first time of ship handling at night and I think it is
great. I will tell you tomorrow how this same skipper pulled a fast one on us.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
