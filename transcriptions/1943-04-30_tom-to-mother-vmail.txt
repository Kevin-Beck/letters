[V-MAIL form, No. 1070 [?]. Censor's stamp: "PASSED BY NAVAL CENSOR" (initials)]

To:    MRS. JOHN A. BECK, KARNS CITY, PENNSYLVANIA, USA
From:  ENS. THOMAS F. BECK, USS LCI (L) 62, c/o POSTMASTER SAN FRANCISCO, CALIF.
       APRIL 30, 1943

Dear Mother,
     The only thing I don't like about these letters (V-Mail) typewritten is
that it takes so much to fill one page. I have a few hours before I go on
watch and perhaps I can think of enough to complete this. As usual I have the
midnight to four watch and to-night I am not very anxious to go on. It isn't a
nice calm moonlite night. And as usual when I sit down to do anything there is
always plenty of interruptions and to-night is no exception. It is tough enough
trying to make a letter coherent and when I have to leave every five minutes
it takes a long time for me to get back in the mood and to what I was trying
to write about. One sure thing, you have to read my letters twice to get at
least an inkling of what I mean.

     I hate to be continuously harping on one subject and I have tried to
refrain heretofore but now I am unable to control myself. I believe you know
what I am refering to - MAIL. I have received a few letters since that one
grand haul I made but there weren't any from home. The last ones I received
was from Aunt Harriet. I still feel, and I can't see where I can be mistaken,
that it isn't your fault and I don't know just who to blame. Some mail has been
getting through to the other men, but then we all feel that it isn't all
coming through. Perhaps there is just too much mail coming here for them
(USNFM [?]) to handle it all. I do believe that Air Mail is a little faster
than V-Mail, but not as dependable. Mail is one thing I can bitch about and get
away with it and, consequently, I blame everything that goes wrong on the
mailman. I do like to know just how everything is progressing and how you all
are at home. I haven't got used to "No news is good news."

     We have had a few transfers on the ship and in most of them we got a
better man than what we gave up. Most of the men we have been getting have
spent a few years in the Navy and are experienced men. They have taken a large
burden from me in supervising the deck work. Also, in handling the men. The
more of this type of men we get the better I like it. We lost our Radioman for
a few weeks and he was the ship's Yeoman. While he is away I have taken over
his job (Yeoman only) and it has given me a chance to brush up on my typing.
If he stays away for two or three months my typing should be fair again or do
you think I should need a stiffer brush.

     Some of the boys on the ship have met buddies from home in these various
ports, but I haven't seen a soul I know except the ones from John Jay who came
down with me. You can tell Izzy that I look up the name of every destroyer I
see and as yet haven't even come close to seeing Don. I think the chances are
very slim of our meeting. Outside of Don I don't know anyone from home that is
in the Navy. At least, I can't think of any one.

     Well, I filled the page and didn't say a thing worthwhile, but I hope my
blabbering isn't a waste of paper and time and that you get some enjoyment out
of attempting to translate it.

[Morse-code-like dots and dashes typed at lower left]
                                        Love to all,
                                             Tom
