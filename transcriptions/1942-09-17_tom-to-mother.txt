                                                  17 September 1942
Dear Mother,
     Pat Hill and her aunt had me out to Hackensack for dinner Sunday. I
really appreciated it very much. Pat is leaving for school this week so I
don't know how I can repay them.

     The talk is now that graduation will be postponed [crossed out: another]
week, Oct. 28, but it is not official. I hear more conflicting stories around
than I have ever heard. I guess because they don't tell us much everyone
elaborate on every little thing. We were issued our uniform lists to-day and
the minimum will cost a small fortune. We have to pay for them, then the
goverment gives us $250 which will cover most of the bill. I think I will be
lost buying clothes without you to supervise. One thing I won't have to worry
about and that is the color and style.

     Speaking of uniforms and such, I hope you didn't go too deep for the

[--- 1942-09-17_tom-to-mother_p02.jpg ---]

pictures. I wish I had the confidence you showed. I will do my best so you
won't have to stick them back in a corner. It seems strange that we have to
make all these plans when we are so uncertain. That must be the Navy policy
because they keep us [crossed out: under] tension all the time. The days seem
to be flying by fast so it won't be long until we know for sure. Exams are
only four weeks from now.

     I am very sorry to hear about Grandma McClung's [?] condition. She seemed
to be in good health when I left. I imagine it is hard on Mr. McClung.

     Speaking of McClung's I am getting anxious to see one of them. I hope he
will remember me.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
