                                                  Dec. 6 - 43
Dear Mum,
     I was quite fortunate to-day and received 8 letters, yours among them.
You thought it would arrive at Thanksgiving time and was [crossed out] only a
few weeks off.

     The memorial dedication sounded like a big affair. The picture of it was
good and I was quite surprised to see something like that in Chicora. I
suppose it is on the corner across from Dick Gaisford.

     You can inform Margie that I have not seen any

[--- 1943-12-06_tom-to-mum_p02.jpg ---]

white civilians since leaving the States. This front differs from the European
one in that respect. She has been very good to me by writing often and I hope
she understands when I don't answer her direct, but through you.

     I hope Henry can find something he likes in [crossed out] Italy. He seemed
discontent with his work in Africa. I hope Bill went across the Atlantic. I
think he will find he work a bit more pleasant there. And I don't think Henry
needs to worry [for awhile, inserted] about being sent across on Armistice
Day. I expect to finish my 18 months out here, have a few months duty on the
Atlantic, and then

[--- 1943-12-06_tom-to-mum_p03.jpg ---]

return out here for the finish. I think my second tour out here will be a long
one.

     I probably mentioned this before. I had dinner with Glenn Scott on his
ship the other night. It was a treat to get away and I really enjoyed it. He
said that his folks intentionally didn't tell him about Jack so I guess I let
the cat out of the bag. He probably is too busy to worry very much about
things like that.

     How do you like being a big director by now. Do you solve all the
problems for them. I imagine that a Christmas gifts for the employees will be
difficult with rationing in effect. Food was

[--- 1943-12-06_tom-to-mum_p04.jpg ---]

something that everyone was satisfied with. How about the dividends, did we
make any money?

     I am glad to hear that you often start the watch with me because I often
think of you and home then. I have lots of time to think during a watch and
often wonder what you are doing at that particular time. Another thing that
causes me some thought [crossed out] is how I will react when I do get home.
There will be so many changes there and no doubt I have changed some myself.
I don't suppose that will make very much difference.

[--- 1943-12-06_tom-to-mum_p05.jpg ---]

     I received two packages to-day. One from Frazier-McCafferty and a fruit
cake from you. I am saving the cake for awhile. The other box had [crossed
out: two] books and a pair of slippers. The slippers are too good to wear on
here because the salt water is rough on every thing. I also received, a few
days ago, a package from Aunt Lyde & one from Isabel.

     I have shot my wad for to-night and also have a few other letters to
answer so shall close.
                                        Love to all,
                                             Tom

[Written at bottom left:] Ens. Thos. F. Beck
