                                                  Saturday night
                                                  Nov 1942
Dear Dad & Mother,
     I am glad you are asking questions because now I have some thing to talk
about that might interest you. I will warn you first that I will try to go
into great detail which no doubt will make a lengthy & confusing letter.

     Your question about the skipper comes first. I will start with the
organization of the crew. There are three officers & 21 enlisted men. The
Commanding officer, called C.O., skipper, captain, or Old Man, is the boss. He
is supposed to be a j.g. but some are Mustangs (men who have come up through
the ranks & seldom get higher than Ensigns). On the L.C.I. he is the navigator
besides he general duties of running the ship. Next in line is the Executive
(Exec.) officer who on the large ships (cruisers & up) usually runs the thing.
The C.O. just supervises & gives general orders. But on the L.C.I. the Exec.
has a few other duties such as gunnery officer,

[--- page 2: 1942-11-28_tom-to-dad-and-mother_p02.jpg ---]

in charge of Damage control & supply. The third officer is Engineering and
his main job is to keep the engines running. Of course all three should know
each others job, especially navigating, because under normal wartime
conditions there probably will be only one on watch at a time. Naturally, the
better our Chiefs are the less work and worry we have.

     The crew is allowed a certain rating for each man and very few have
reached their allowed rating yet. Our crew is quite fortunate so far in that
all men have a rating, no apprentice seaman. We did get an unfortunate break
in one man. He was a gunners mate and had seen action in Pearl Harbor,
Midway, Solomons and in a few other skirmishes. I was expecting him to relieve
me of a lot of work as gunnery officer but I didn't count too strongly on him.
I had a feeling he was too good and they took him away to a L.S.T. The
ordnance department was after him as an instructor and he was to be
interviewed a couple of times. I reluctantly send and each

[--- page 3 (marked "-2-"): 1942-11-28_tom-to-dad-and-mother_p03.jpg ---]

time was surprised to see him return. Just before he shoved off for the L.S.T.
he told me that officer didn't show up for the interview and he intentionally
stayed away after that. He said that he didn't want to leave the crew. It gave
me a strange feeling - I don't know whether it was me or the crew that he like.
I hope it was both. (Could you follow he through all that.)

     That is the organization - partially filled - one officer & 14 men. One
of the attributes necessary to an officer, I found out, is not listed by John
Paul Jones. He gives a lot of them but left out one - Mother. I thought I was
young & didn't know much, but there are some young kids here that have plenty
of troubles. I really can't help them but don't like to say no. I do the best
I can which isn't very good, but the crew seems to be satisfied. All of them
are as anxious as I am to get aboard our own ship.

     I was going to tell you about our trip out into the bay but that would
take too long so will save it for

[--- page 4: 1942-11-28_tom-to-dad-and-mother_p04.jpg ---]

tomorrow. To fill the page I will tell you about my room mates & room.

     The room is on top side of one of the barracks, orignally meant for
chiefs. There are five single & two upper & lower bunks. We have our private
bath & shower but have to use the enlisted mens laundry. I like that because
so far I have spent most of my time in there talking to the men & not washing
my clothes. Our forces here have dwindled done to three. One, Coopersmith, is
a Jew - he is from Chicago. The other one is Baker, from Mo., received he
Masters in English at Yale, & has taught English & was a reporter. He is a
smart boy, is well read & is still a teacher. He may not realize it but he is
teaching me a few things about English, Lit., etc. I have often read about
fellows sitting around & discussing big problems & doubted it all. We don't
get into any thing deep, but since I have been here I have read quite a few
articles & editorials and often ask his opinion [on] them. I think he is very
interesting to talk to.
     Will write again tomorrow.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
