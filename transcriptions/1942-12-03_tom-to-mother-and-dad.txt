                                                  12-3-42
Dear Mother & Dad,
     I am sorry I skipped a few days between letters. I don't have the free
time now as I did before. This week evening classes were started which
interfere with my correspondence. As I told Betty I could start writing
letters in class but that would disrupt my sleep. Even now I haven't much
time to spend on this letter.

     I received your gift to-day and it will come in very handy. I also
received a present from Hank & Harriet - a pair of sox. That makes five pair
now. If this cold wave sticks I will be tempted to wear all five pair at the
same time. It is strange to have the winter weather without snow.

     For some unknown reason the roads were oil. The ground is too hard for it
to penetrate and [crossed out] now there is little left on the roads and every
building is covered. It is a good way

[--- 1942-12-03_tom-to-mother-and-dad_p02.jpg ---]

to oil the floor.

     I know as much about Christmas leave as I do anything else here -
nothing. All I can do is hope I get one. I have received two gifts so far -
from Hank & Harriet & the Womans Missionary Society. I am afraid if I get many
more I won't have room to pack them. I am crowded for space now. There is
really nothing I need now so if there are any gifts for me please keep them
until I get home. I don't want to leave a lot of stuff behind like I did at
John Jay.

     After a few practical days on the boats life here has again become
normal. I am in my third week of communications. Just to relieve the monotony
the[re] is a little seamanship thrown in.

     To-day is some sort of an [crossed out] anniversary for me. One moon ago
I landed here all set for two weeks of hard, but practical, work. It is
strange how quickly one can change ones mind and adjust oneself to the
surroundings.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
