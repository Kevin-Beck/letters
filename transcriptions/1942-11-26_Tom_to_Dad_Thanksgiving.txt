                                                  Thanksgiving
Dear Dad,
     You got me in a bad habit expecting a letter from you every day. I didn't
receive any news from home for a couple of days and I thought you probably had
some trouble again. Your came to-day and everything is fine. I realize it is
as difficult for you to find enough to fill a letter every day as it is for me
and I won't expect so many from now on. I hope you have more to say about your
thanksgiving than I have.

     It was just another day here except for one big meal which was really
delicious. Our class was scheduled for some practical work. We went [to, on]
one of the small boats (105 ft) and Baker (my roommate & bud now) was told to
get one one of the other boats. It had just started to pull out and Baker
jumped & missed. He was very heavily clothed but was fortunate

[--- 421126Pg2.jpg ---]

that he only had to swim a few strokes. He must have taken the training more
serious than I did - I never never but believe that all commands should be
obeyed.

     The work was very interesting to-day. First, I was the helmsman for awhile
there wasn't much to that. All I did was put the rudder where I was ordered
to. We had maneuver-board drill in which I had to give the commands. A box was
thrown over and I had to stop the ship with the box amidships and the
starboard. I was doing fine until I tried to stop. I got mixed up and missed
the box. Only one man did it right and he was an old timer. I learned a lot
this morning and hope to get out again soon.

     I ate too much turkey at noon and had to stay in my room this afternoon.
I also had to console Baker. He is just a little fellow and didn't [have] a
dry uniform or shoes and he wore mine. He looked a little queer. He and I went
to a movie this evening.

     I am beginning to think you are right about this place - I might as

[--- 421126PG3.jpg ---]

well take it easy while I can. I imagine I will look back on this as a good
time. My pet peeve about this place is the way the enlisted men are treated -
Most of them are used to getting some liberty and being paid and this is too
much of a contrast. I tried once to get clothes & shoes for my crew but I
couldn't do any good. I try to tell them that it will be different when we get
off of here and I don't think they believe me. I really think it will.

     I thought the Butler Hospital was for casualties and I can't see why a
sick man needs a dance. If only the staff is there they have an easy job,
especially if it is like the staff here. All members of the staff here,
officers & men, get the evenings & week-ends off. I suppose that makes the
people feel better when they help any service men.

     I agree with you that some one type of people seem to get soft jobs. I
suppose the reason is that they aren't ashamed to ask for them. I suppose the
reason I don't like one guy here is that the only time I see him

[--- 421126pG4.jpg ---]

is when I pay him my linen fee. I had to laugh the other night when some of my
crew started complaining about the Jews. It doesn't take much education to get
on to them.

     Another man from our barracks is leaving to-morrow. That leaves only three
out of nine. They are moving some men.

     I haven't had a letter from Mother for a long time. She wrote to me at
John Jay when I addressed the letters home to her. Perhaps she is a little
jealous of you. From now on I will address my letters to both of you and
perhaps both of you will [crossed out] answer.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
