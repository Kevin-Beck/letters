                                                  19 September 1942
Dear Mother,
     I have a little time before my last class and then am ready for the
weekend. I have a date this week-end and that is about all I know about. One
of the fellows on the deck is an altar boy and got in good with the priest.
There are a couple of Power's models who belong to the Parish and the Priest
made the arrangements. I don't know how I got in on it. I never heard of the
Power's [crossed out] models but from what I hear they are suppose to be quite
beautiful.

     I don't think you are being fair with Hickory. We have always bragged
what a big, healthy baby he is and [crossed out] now he is expected to carry

[--- 420919pg2.jpg ---]

all his weight. He is just playing it smart by letting other people do the
work for awhile. He realizes he has to carry all that weight for a long time.
I bet he could walk if he wanted to.

     I received Isabel's letter this morning and I gather she had a good time.
She is quite right that the service men don't know what is going on. We
receive no information other than what we read in the news papers and I only
read the Sunday paper.

     For once I can say I think they are easing up a little. It seemed that
way to me this last week but I don't think it will last long. The only nice
thing about extending graduation until the 21st is that we can either go
slower or review. Either way I think we can learn much more than the previous
method.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
