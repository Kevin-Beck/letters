                                                  7/6/42
Dear Mother,

     I just finished dinner and I think I have a free evening so I can give a
few of the details so far. The Pullman wasn't so bad but I couldn't sleep. I
met a fellow from Indiana, Pa. in the morning and we came in to-gether.
Yesterday we spent the whole day getting uniforms and getting located. Mine
doesn't fit so badly except for size 15 1/2 - 3 [?] shirt and 38 shorts.

     We drilled for about 3 hours to-day and it wasn't so bad. Where I get
most of my exercise is climbing the stairs. I am on the 13th floor and go up
and down about eight times a day.

     Our schedule thus far has been up at six, breakfast,
[line cut off at bottom of scan]

[--- 1942-07-06_tom-to-mother_p02.jpg ---]

back to the room, drilling, wash for lunch, lunch, back upstairs, more
drilling etc. Every time we finish anything we have to return to our rooms.
My legs are either going to get stronger or break.

     I have only met a few of the fellows and they all seemed to be O.K. They
come from all over the country. The Southern boys because of their drawl
stand out above the rest.

     We had an 1 1/2 hr. liberty this afternoon and I went for a walk. I was
hunting a barber shop and walk most of the time trying to find one that
wasn't full - I couldn't do it so I am going to try to-morrow if we get a
liberty. Our bounds are from 110th St. to 122nd St. and

[--- 1942-07-06_tom-to-mother_p03.jpg (marked "-2-") ---]

[Printed letterhead: eagle, shield and anchors, "UNITED STATES NAVY"]

from Riverside Dr. to Morningside Dr. I don't know [crossed out] if this is
enforced over the week-ends. They also shut the beer off.

     They talk to us in Navy terms and half of the time I haven't the
slightest idea what they mean. The floors are decks, stairs - ladders,
basement - hold, eating - mess, and John - head.

     I am not sure of this but I think the first course will last only 3
weeks and where we go next depends upon our grades.

     If you are thinking of coming up you had better wait until I see just
how I can entertain you. There is no place
[line cut off at bottom of scan]

[--- 1942-07-06_tom-to-mother_p04.jpg ---]

sounds it will hardly be worthwhile coming. The canteen has run out of ink
and there isn't any desk in our room yet so I am using the box. Aunt Helen
gave me [sentence left unfinished]
     That is all for now. Will write again soon.
     I hope [crossed out] your 4th is better than the prospects of ours.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
