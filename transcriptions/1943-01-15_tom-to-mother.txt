                                   [later hand: 1/15/43?]   1/15/42 [sic]
Dear Mother,
     Received your letter this morning. I don't know where Sprecher is now and
I am not sure of his Pittsburgh address but I think it is 5 Highland Court. I
saw a friend of Chet's down at Norfolk and he had a clipping of the wedding.
It said that Chet just came back from the Pacific Fleet. The closet he has
come to the Pacific was when he was told he would pick his ship up down here.
Even that was changed and he is still back East. I haven't seen him since he
left Solomons.

     Speaking of the Pacific, my chances of sailing are grea[t]. I really
think that will be th[e] [corner of page folded over]

[--- 1943-01-15_tom-to-mother_p02.jpg ---]

the direction I am going. It will be a few months before I leave the country.
As soon as we go to Galveston we start using the Postmaster address but don't
let it worry you. If we leave very soon I know we won't get far because our
skipper knows less about the ship than I do. We will have to practice before
we can go very far.

     I hate to admit this but there is hardly any work at all to do. We have
checked a few publications and that is all. The ship is covered with work men
and there isn't room to turn around. There is an office where we are supposed
to report every day and to do our work. Mostly we just sit around and talk or
read the newspapers. Yesterday some

[--- 1943-01-15_tom-to-mother_p03.jpg (marked "-2-") ---]

Lt. came in to give us a pep talk. He gave the same type talk that is typical
of Solomons. It disgusted all of us. I found out later that he was the man
that anchored in a fairway at Little Creek and the Cape Charles Ferry hit him.
If his ship had sunk he would probably be a full commander now but since it
only had a hole in it he only received a position on the staff. This morning
the Commanding Officer kicked us out of the office with the remark "This is a
hardworking organization." and so I am catching up on some letter writing. If
it wasn't for the efficiency of the employees here this place would be
miserable. I even reported to a stenographer rather than the Commanding
Officer because she is the only one that knows what is going on.

     I was invited out to dinner Wednesday evening and the people were very
nice. They had a very cute daughter. She is only 3 1/2 yrs.

[--- 1943-01-15_tom-to-mother_p04.jpg ---]

old. I was invited back next Wednesday and they are going to a Basketball
game. It is such a contrast to Norfolk. I also looked in the phone book for
the McMahons & O'Conners but there were too many of them so will wait until
their addresses arrive.

     Perhaps it would be best not to tell where I am going or what I said
about the organization here. Some people might get the idea that the Navy is
like that everyplace. It is only in the Ambiguous Force, no similarity to the
Navy, that it is really Snafu.

     I have been thinking about my income tax and perhaps John should pay it
while I still have some money. My only income is from the Co. trust, & Agency
account. I worked for awhile last Spring and my pay in the Service. That would
be $21 as A.S., $195 as Midshipman, & $300 as Ensign. Also $150 Uniform
Gratuity. Even tho they don't pay I guess it is considered income. Let me know
what details you need.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
