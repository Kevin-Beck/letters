                                                  Feb. 11, 1943
Dear Mother,
     I suppose there has been quite an elapse of time between letters but you
will have to expecte that from now on. It is still difficult to find any news.

     I came aboard this ship the night before sailing. I felt quite green &
unsure of myself but the skipper said not to worry that I would learn the hard
way and I did. I stood the 12 to 4 watches and the first one came at midnight.
I never thought that the first time I would run a ship would be in the open sea
let alone in the dark.

     The first few days were a little rough but, surprisingly, I didn't get
sick. I didn't feel very well but I really think it was the sudden change of
routine. I stand watch on 4 & off 8 hours plus trying to navigate & other work.
The days were hot and it took the ship a long time to cool off in the evenings.
The ship

[--- 430211pg2.jpg ---]

is darkened which cuts off a lot of ventilation. The nights were hot & I was
nervous so I think I would have felt rather low even on shore. Just as I
[crossed out] was getting used to it all we arrived here and are now at anchor.
I imagine there will be a lot more to come.

     It is strange what one can do if one has to. The skipper had me doing most
of the navigation work and I never took a sextant sight before. I did what the
book said to do and he still thinks our positions were more accurate than the
lead ship's. It gave me some assurance. I still have to learn how to find the
different stars. I don't know them yet.

     I am going ashore as soon as I can get liberty & bury my nose in some suds.
A bottle of beer would sure taste good these afternoons. I can['t] help
mentioning the weather as this is the coldest month of the year.

[--- 430211pg3.jpg (marked "-2-") ---]

     I suppose my mail will catch up with eventually, even that sent to the
345. If you sent the whites I imagine I will get them next winter if & when I
get back to the states. As for mail I believe I will limit my correspondence to
you & possibly one or two other women. It will be difficult finding enough
allowable material for one letter. If I can find a station I will try the
V-mail. It will be a lot faster & surer.

     I have a couple "thank-you" notes to write so will close this one.

     Don't worry about me. Just keep a soft bed in a cool spot ready for me and
I will be back home in no time.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
