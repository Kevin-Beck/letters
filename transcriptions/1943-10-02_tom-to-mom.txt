                                                  Saturday
                                                  Oct. 2, 1943
Dear Mum,
     I really should keep a record of when I write because I usually have no
idea how far apart my letters are. Of course, this time I know it has been
quite a few days since I last wrote and I am sorry.

     Received your letter of the 24th to-day. It is the one with the farming
pictures. I especially like the one of you. The only other one I have of you
is that one taken while waiting for the street car at Cannonsburg. You looked
a little thin & tired. In this picture you look more like yourself. Whether
you like it or not, your face is much fuller and I think it is a better
picture.

[--- 1943-10-02_tom-to-mom_p02.jpg ---]

I hope the other pictures weren't in that batch that was lost. I haven't
received them yet.

     You and Aunt Bess are much better at time than I am. It took me 15
minutes to check your figures and I agree with them. I never thought much
about it before but does seem strange.

     It sounded like a grand party at Moshers. Were you celebrating anything in
particular. You must play poker like I do. John & George inherited their
poker sense from Dad, I got mine from you. I very seldom win. I am thankful
that I can either take it or leave it alone. Schwalbach, Hofmann, and myself
play some complicated game every now and

[--- 1943-10-02_tom-to-mom_p03.jpg ---]

then. It is a German game - American name is Sheepshead. Practically every
thing is trump and some low cards are high - all mixed up. The stakes are
small - I won the last two nights. Net gain is about 50¢. Sort of got side
tracked. How is Adam & Dorthy? I haven't heard from them for a long time.
Suppose I will have to write to them again.

     I am afraid Hofmann and I aren't related. We had an argument the other
night and I still think he spells his name wrong - should be Hoffman. He is a
bit obstinate and won't agree.

     Schwalbach was just reading a letter from his wife. She received (or
bought) a table cloth and napkins worth $50 and he thinks it is a lot

[--- 1943-10-02_tom-to-mom_p04.jpg ---]

of dough to put into something to wipe his mouth. He now uses the back of his
hand. I told him he will never see them - she will keep them tucked away for
some special occasion which never arrives.

     I received a nice letter from Aunt Harriet the other day. She said she is
keeping one eye open for a mate for me. The type she described will be hard
to find but I hope she has some luck.

     Sorry this is all for now. Will write often when possible.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom

(Expect to send a check home soon)

[Written at bottom left:] [En]s. Thos. F. Beck
