                                                  Sept. 5, '43
Dear Mom,
     To-day is Sunday and for once I took advantage of it. Worked for awhile
this morning but took a few hours off in the afternoon to read a book. I always
have a guilty feeling when I read or write letters during the day - don't even
like to wash clothes on "company time." There are days when I am not busy and
I spend the time looking for work. Of course there are plenty of days like
to-morrow when I am busy from dawn to dusk. But that is life.

     We have been fortunate the last few days - the movie boat has been
stopping here. I very seldom attend - haven't gotten used to the Navy theaters
where there is standing room only in selected spots where

[--- 430905pg2.jpg (marked "-2-") ---]

it is impossible to see or hear. My feet and neck always bother me after an
evenings entertain at the theater. I am content to stay aboard and let the
other men go.

     The news to-night was good. The boys are finally in Europe and I hope the
keep moving fast. The Japs are beginning to believe it is worthwhile to shoot
and run to live to shoot another day. That other day is going to be a tough
one for us. At least we have them running. It would be nice if those who ran
would explain the simplicity and the finer points of the act to the other
Japs and there by create a mass cross-island run to Tokyo where we will be
able to get them all at once. Anyway it is a pleasant thought. Contrary to
Public Opinion the Japs are smart.

[--- 430905pg3.jpg (marked "-3-") ---]

     It hardly seems possible that the day after to-morrow school will start
again. The summer really flew by, at least it did for me. It seems like only
last week you were writing about the spring rains and its effect on the
gardens. I suppose you will start harvesting soon. When the harvest starts you
days will be full and I hope the basement will be likewise. I also remember
the comments about the cold weather which will be with you soon again.

     Haven't had mail for a few days so I can't stretch this letter any longer.
My topics come mainly from your letters.

     All personal subjects have remained unchanged.
                                        Love to all,
                                             Tom

[Printed at lower left:] Pardon the envelope; just my brain working over time.
[Written at bottom left:] Beck, T.F., ENS.
