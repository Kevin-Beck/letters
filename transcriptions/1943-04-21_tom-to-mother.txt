                                                  4/21/43
Dear Mother,

     In my last letter I just about exhausted my supply of news and now I
don't know what to write about. I thought I would be getting mail regularly
here but haven't received any since that one time. It is much easier to write
after receiving a letter even tho' I do write back the same thing.

     It is rather foolish to say I am fine and dandy because if I wasn't I
wouldn't be writing. Most of the letters I censor start out with "Just a few
lines to let you [crossed out] no I am fine and sure hope you all are the
same." I might start mine that way from now on. At least the last part is
always my hope.

     Everyone I write to should realize by this time what a poor speller I am,
but I have found out that I am not the worst. I have even found some writing
worst than mine. I think the Navy should [small insertion above line:
"(any as bad as me)" ?] make all sailors write with a typewriter.

     We had a stroke of good luck the other day. We received in a transfer a
cox

[--- 1943-04-21_tom-to-mother_p02.jpg (marked "-2-") ---]

(I am using this paper because of Air Mail limitations and didn't realize that
I couldn't write on both sides.) He has been in the Reserves for 15 years and
he knows his Navy which is a big help to all of us.

     We have all been working quite strenuously since we have been here and the
last two days were rather tough on me. I spend most of my time in the conning
tower and while there I can't sit down. It is tiresome especially on the legs.
I was planning on going to bed early tonight but no one else is going ashore so
I will have to go myself in order to get this letter posted. I am ready now but
there is no sign of a liberty boat. If it does come around I probably won't get
a chance to mail this for a week.

The boat is here.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
