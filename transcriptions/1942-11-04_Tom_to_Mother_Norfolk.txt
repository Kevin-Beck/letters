                                                  Wednesday
                                                  1943 [sic]
Dear Mother,
     We are still here at Norfolk waiting. No one seems to know when or where -
(I was called down for a muster - 8 hrs later) I leave to-morrow morning for
Soloman's Island and I am glad the suspence is over.

     The few days here were easy but monotonous. We had two hours of drill in
the morning, afternoon, and night. I heard that the commanding officer
couldn't get near the bar at the officer's club so he ordered all of the young
officers to take a 2 hr hike every night at 8:00 o'clock. It does ruin the
evening. This place we are staying in is very nice. It is as nice as any hotel
and only costs 25¢ a night. Meals costs from 20¢ to 30¢.

[--- Z 43 AC Pg2.jpg ---]

     I have heard many conflicting stories about the Solomans and none sound
very pleasant. I will have to wait & find out for myself. All with the mud,
rain, & tarpaper huts we have to do our own laundry. If it is true I imagine
my clothes will be able to walk to any laundry before I wash them.

     I had a good offer to-night but was afraid to try it. Chet's friend asked
me what all I knew about gasoline. They are having trouble get the gas from
the storage tanks to the planes and want a man who knows some thing about
transportation. It would be a wonderful job but I think they want an engineer
not a C.& F.'er.

     As I understand it my address is the same as the one I send. All mail is
forwarded. Don't send any clothes until I tell you. Will write when I get
settled.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom
