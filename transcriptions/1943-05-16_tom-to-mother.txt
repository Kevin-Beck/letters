                                                  Sunday Night
                                                  May 16, 1943
Dear Mother,
     I received a letter from Betty to-day (Apr. 28) and from it I gathered you
are just beginning to receive my mail. I was hoping that at least my mail was
getting through o.k. We should be satisfied that it is getting through to both
of us.

     So at last John has made up his mind. It really wasn't much of a surprise
to me. I would like to be home for the big day but I am afraid that if he
waits for me it will be a long engagement. I will be satisfied just as long
as you keep me informed. Also in the same subject I understand Uncle John
finally gave in. Libby stuck to her guns for a long time. Is she
[line(s) cut off at bottom of scan]

[--- 1943-05-16_tom-to-mother_p02.jpg (marked "-2-") ---]

staying at home or going back with Buddy?

     When speaking of weddings one thinks of clothes so I might just as well
confirm what I said the other day. I am sending all my woolen stuff, raincoat,
whites, and a few miscellaneous thing to you. You can either stow them, wear
them, or throw them away. If I have any use for them in the future I can buy
some more because no doubt if and when I want them I won't have time to wait
for delivery. They are packed in two containers - mailing case & John's
suitcase. I am to ship them to-morrow and you can expect them in 4 or 5 weeks.
They are just excess clothes not needed
[line(s) cut off at bottom of scan]

[--- 1943-05-16_tom-to-mother_p03.jpg (marked "-3-") ---]

with me physically - mentally (?)
(See Note) This check is the same one I received upon arriving here. I can't
cash it and don't need money and can't see any reason for keeping it aboard.
Either use it to buy some nice big juicy steaks or put it in the bank. If my
financial adviser (John) so advises buy a bond. Perhaps better still, buy some
bonded.

     I haven't listened to the radio for a few weeks but I am told that Africa
is just about de-loused. That is good news to me in two ways - First, I am
glad to hear of any front being taken; second, the fewer the fronts the
concentrated they become. I trust they are still hearing from Henry.

     Speaking of fronts I hear from sources outside the family that Marge is
becoming a [beautiful, partly cut off] young lady. She was
[line(s) cut off at bottom of scan]

[--- 1943-05-16_tom-to-mother_p04.jpg (marked "-4-") ---]

well on her way the last time I saw her. She has a birthday around the 27th
of this month if my memory is correct. Strange how I can remember birthdays
when I am in a locality where gifts are unobtainable.

     I did buy a little gadget the other day with no particular reason in mind
except perhaps they are scarce as hen's teeth. It is part of our uniform. When
in khaki we are allowed, some places ordered, to wear overseas caps. This
device goes on the cap so one can tell a Naval officer from a buck private. I
personally don't like the caps so I haven't any use for the device. The Eng.
Officer sent his home to his wife. Civilians are not permitted to wear them so
that is why I have hesitated sending mine to you. Also, you no doubt have
enough jewelry. But if

[--- 1943-05-16_tom-to-mother_p05.jpg (marked "-5-") ---]

I do decide to send it and forget to tell you, you probably can wear it
because I doubt if anyone around home knows a damn thing about Navy Regs. It
is only hearsay as far I am concerned.

     It is 2300 and a very busy day tomorrow to look forward to.
                                        All my Love,
                                             Tom

Note: Due to lapse of memory or carelessness I can't find the check. I can't
remember whether or not I sent it to you before. Will make a thorough seach
soon and will let you know.
