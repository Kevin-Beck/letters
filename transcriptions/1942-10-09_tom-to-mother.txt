[Printed letterhead: seal "U.S. NAVAL RESERVE MIDSHIPMEN'S SCHOOL", "NEW YORK, N.Y."]

                                                  9 October 1942
Dear Mother,
     There really isn't much to write about now. I am mostly after
information. How are you planning on coming here? Most of the fellows are
making reservations to get home and if you are coming by train I wonder if I
should do something about a ticket. I don't know how far in advance the
tickets must be purchased but I think it shouldn't be difficult to get
passage to Pittsburgh. If you are planning on driving there is nothing to
worry about.

     Unless you have already asked someone forget about extra tickets. They
are hard to get. The other fellows are more forehanded than I am and asked
for extra cards a long time ago. They were more sure of themselves. We will
be issued one extra pass which will admit one to a room in which the

[--- 1942-10-09_tom-to-mother_p02.jpg ---]

exercises will be broadcasted. I doubt if that will appeal to many. If you
have already ask someone let me know and I will hunt further.

     This is for Dad. Mr. Wasson [?] said he sent you a copy of his letter. I
signed the two forms and sent them back. We can settle the other matter while
I am home.

     Tommorrows review will be fun. We are trying something new. Before we
marched in companies and couldn't [get] them all on the field at one time. To
make the units more compact we will march as battalion. The men in the back
couldn't hear the company commander's orders and now with the battalion
officer giving them it is a mess. It would look good if we can do it because
there will be about 400 men marching shoulder to shoulder. It had better be
good because an Admiral is reviewing us. I finally made the front ranks for
this formation.
                                        Love,
                                             Tom

P.S. We have to get up at 5:30 now and run around the drill field before
breakfast. I will be glad to see graduation.
