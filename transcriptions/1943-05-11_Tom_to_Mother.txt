                                                  May 11, 1943
Dear Mother,
     I wrote a short note last night so I would have at least one letter going
out in the mail to-day. It was the first chance we have had for a week to send
mail and everytime we send mail we expect some in return. I am hoping for some
to-day.

     I just finished rereading your Apr. 7 letter and it was just like a fresh
one. I have been quite busy after first receiving it and didn't get a chance
to reread it until to-day.

     I just asked the Engineering Officer what branch of the Navy V-12 is and
he said the Waves. If he is correct, I think George made an excellent choice.
I might try for a transfer myself. Whatever it is I hope George makes it and
that he will like his work.

[--- 430511pg2.jpg (marked "-2-") ---]

     I was sorry to hear about Dick being in Sick Bay. He certainly seems to
get more than his share of tough breaks. He was fortunate to get to room with
Armstrong. I think it is much easier picking up an old friendship than always
making new ones. It would be nice if he could get an assignment in Slippery
Rock. That was the first I have heard of Armstrong's whereabouts since I left
State.

     Thanks a lot for giving those books in my name. I personally think Zane
Grey's books are a lot better than a lot of the modern best sellers. Of course
I like Thorne Smith and Balzac but I doubt if the School Board would
appreciate them. What picture of Dad are they going to use in the
school-house? And speaking of pictures, is there

[--- 430511pg3.jpg (marked "-3-") ---]

a small one of the family you could send me. A miniature of the last group
picture we had taken.

     Right now I think it is foolish of you to keep the cottage open in hopes
that I might get any use out of it this summer. I will be quite fortunate if I
get home before Christmas. At least I am not planning on anything before then.
Of course it would be nice to leave the cottage open just in case you want go
up for a week-end to cool off and relax.

     I am glad they are still hearing from Henry. I haven't heard any news from
the African front but imagine that there is still plenty of fighting going on
there.

     Don't get frightened if you receive most of my winter clothing in the
mail. It has been suggested that we get rid of our blues, whites,

[--- 430511pg4.jpg (marked "-4-") ---]

and other clothing that we don't need. If I can't find stowage for them here I
will probably send them home. I will let you know before hand.

     I have some other mail to answer and I hope some to read when the mail
orderly gets back so I had better close now. I shall write again soon.
                                        Love to all,
                                             Tom

P.S. Tell Jackie I said "hello".
