                                                  April 7, 1943
Dear Unc,
     I left the States about six weeks ago and several times attempted to
write to you. Now, I can't remember whether or not I did write so I will
pretend that I didn't and start from the first. I guess I need a secretary.
Some of this may be old stuff to you.

     The ship I was orginally assigned to was commissioned the last of January
and then I thought I would finally get the training I needed and wanted. I did
make a few trips out in the Bay but never ran the thing. I was to wait until
the skipper learned then it was my turn. That arrangement suited me fine but
not our Commander. One of the other ships lost and officer so I replaced him.
The next day we left the States.

     My first watch was the midnight to four and I was really nervous. I didn't
make any serious mistakes

[--- 1943-04-07_tom-to-unc_p02.jpg ---]

and finished the trip more a sailor than when I started. The only scare I had
was when the rudder stuck but the other ships missed me.

     Our voyage is cut into about five trips the first which was long, the
second damn long, and the rest rather short. These short ones are tough as we
stand 4 on and 4 off. I still stand the midnight to four which I am beginning
to detest. I have to fight to keep awake even when I walk around and I don't
dare sit down.

     As long as I have been on board to-day was the first time I handled the
ship in a harbor. I was the only officer aboard to-day when a storm came up
and the anchor started to slip. I had to move or go aground so I moved. I hated
to do it because these ships a[re] very hard to handle in a strong wind. None
of the men jumped overboard so that gave me a little confindence. I did some
fancy maneuvering but got the ship where I wanted it.

[--- 1943-04-07_tom-to-unc_p03.jpg ---]

     I can't say much about the ports [crossed out] we have been to. None are
like the states. A couple are really outposts and the worst part about all of
them is that beer is rationed. One was dry and I tried some native brew made
from bananas which was very tasty but potent. It was like prohibition sneaking
through the woods to some old shack and smacking our lips over some rot-gut. I
haven't been ashore here yet but I think this place will be more civilized. It
looks like a fair sized city from out here in the bay.

     About the only thing I had to show for my trip was six weeks growth on my
face. It was jet black and about an inch long compared to my head which is
blond and 1/2 inch long. I was a damn peculiar looking thing. But lately I have
been wearing

[--- 1943-04-07_tom-to-unc_p04.jpg ---]

a helmet and the chin strap and chin wiskers raised hell with my chin so I
shaved. I also have a rather deep suntan - all I wear is shorts and shoes and
the sun has been hot.

     That is about all I am permitted to write and I admit it isn't very
interesting.

     I haven't heard from any one since January and I am hoping that all of
you are fine. Before I left the rest of the family assured me that Mother was
getting along fine. I have never been called upon to do any thing extremely
difficult but [crossed out: that] the night I left Mother was the toughest day
I have had. I am so glad she is fortunate to have so many of her family near
her.

     I don't know when I will get an opprotunity to write again but I will make
another attempt in the future.
                                        Love to all,
                                             Tom
