                                                  USS LCI(L) 62
                                                  March 12, 1943
Dear Mother,
     We are still at sea but I have a few minutes so will write as much as
I can before I forget everything. When I was sure which direction we were
going I had the clippers run over my head. It is a lot cooler this way but
at times a little inconvenient. I forgot and went without a hat for just a
a few minutes and the top of my head got sunburned. It didn't bother me so
much until it started to peel. The only way I could get rid of the dead skin
was to brush my hair with an old toothbursh. At the same time I got my head
clipped I quit shaving and now my beard is longer and blacker than my head.
At first it was tough looking in the mirror but I am getting used to it by no[w]
One afternoon it was extremely warm so I stood my watch with my shirt open
and burned my belly. It peeled too. I tried taking it easy with my back and
got fairly brown but I am afraid it is going to peel also. I think I have sh[ed]
about enough skin for this season.
     A few days ago I was just an ordinary pollywog but now I am an old moss-
back in King Neptune Court. In other words I crossed the Equator. There was[n't]
much to it--we crossed it at night and I couldn't see a thing. I guess it is
blacked out for the duration. I would hate to travel very much near the
equator because we had a tough time getting a noon sight on the sun. It
was all most directly overhead and we could bring it down any place on the
horizon. I could never tell which way to look. The sights are comming along
much better now.
(time out for chow--will add more at first oppotunity)

     I have a few minutes before General Quarters so will try again. Even
after a couple of weeks it will be hard to find much of interest to say. The
watches are getting very tiresome and nerve wracking. It is four on and eight
off every day plus a lot of other work and it is difficult for me to get used
to sleeping a couple of hours at a time. By the time I get accustomed to the
routine we will hit port and back to normal life for awhile. I don't expect t[o]
[be] parked in one spot for any length of time. I think we will keep moving from
now on. One thing I hope now is that when I come back I come back as a passen[ger].

Mar. 15
     This is somewhat like the life at Solomons. I put a batch of sox and
underwear to soak and now I can't find time to wash them. I am saving one
clean suit of khaki so if we do hit a port I will be able to leave the ship
for awhile. It is quite difficult to wash in salt water.
     We were supposed to be close enough to an island yesterday that we could
see some mountains and I have been waiting for a week just to see land. We
were a little behind schedule and passed tham last night so I will have to
wait a few more days. Maybe it is better this way when we will dock when
we see our first land in weeks.
     I heard part of a news broadcast last night--the first since we left.
Couldn't catch much because of static. A station in Oklahoma comes in over
the radio just before they sign off and for awhile after coming on in the
morning. I heard just enough to wonder how the war is progressing.
     I am glad I saved some old letters. I ran across some in my suitcase
the other day and have been reading them. I keep wondering when my mail
will catch up with me. I expect a lot when it does. (General Quarters)
