                                                  Jan. 18, 1943
Dear Mother,

     Some day I am going to find another place besides a bed to write a
letter. Practically every place I have been we have to use the bed for both
table & chair. My writing is bad enough and trying to write on a bed makes it
worse.

     I forgot to send Sprecher's letter back in my last letter. I have
received quite a few Christmas cards since I have been here and it is
[crossed out: quite] a difficult task answering them. A lot of the mail on [?]
the #218 is finally catching up with me.

     You have mentioned a trip to Florida. If things turn out the way I expect

[--- 430118pg3.jpg ---]

to happen.

     I am really not spending all the money I wired [?] for on a good time. I
had to buy a uniform, sun glasses, etc. I have loaned some to the other men
who haven't be paid either. Also every [crossed out] time we go to town we
have to stay at a hotel which is well known and naturally high priced. The
people are very nice to us here but it cost us a lot of money to be
entertained. I won't have to bother you as soon as we are paid which will be
soon, I hope.

     I told you about the girl I met here but don't worry about it. We will be
leaving soon. & she is very nice but I hear that a sailor has a girl in every

[--- 430118pg4.jpg ---]

port. I guess I will have to get use to that.

     My biggest disappointment in Texas is the weather. It has rained almost
every day and then last Saturday the sun came out and was quite warm. On
Sunday a Norther, what they call a cold spell, hit here and it has been down
around freezing. It is surprising the number of men that caught colds. I have
a slight cold myself. I am afraid that I will have a long wait if I wait for
the sun to cure me.

     Let me know what you think of a trip to California and I will see what I
can do about it.

                                        Love,
                                             Tom
